Wagga Wagga sits at 147 m elevation on the Murrumbidgee floodplain, where the alluvial profile often masks deep residual clays. Over 68,000 people now live in the city, and new subdivisions keep pushing into areas with highly reactive soils. Footing performance here depends on getting the bearing stratum right — not just the allowable pressure, but the expected movement under seasonal moisture change. Our shallow foundation design work starts with test pits to log the upper 3–4 m, then connects those observations to AS 2870 site classification. For raft slabs on Class H or E sites, we also run Atterberg limits to quantify shrink-swell potential before sizing stiffening beams. The goal is a foundation that handles both static load and the Wagga Wagga clay reactivity cycle without excessive heave or settlement.

A footing is only as good as the soil directly beneath it — in Wagga Wagga, that means verifying reactivity class before concrete pours.

Technical details of the service in Wagga Wagga

The core of our verification is the bearing capacity calculation using the general shear equation with shape, depth, and inclination factors from AS 4678. We extract undisturbed samples from the bearing level — usually 0.8–1.5 m below finished surface in Wagga Wagga's western suburbs — and test them for undrained shear strength via unconfined compression or lab vane. Settlement is checked separately using elastic half-space methods, with modulus values calibrated against SPT N60 from the adjacent borehole. For sites near the Murrumbidgee where the water table sits within 2 m of the surface, we account for buoyant unit weight and reduced effective stress. The output is a set of allowable bearing pressures with a factor of safety not less than 3.0, plus predicted total and differential settlement figures that the structural engineer can use directly. Every report references the specific AS 2870 site class determined from the field investigation.

Critical ground factors in Wagga Wagga

Wagga Wagga expanded rapidly after the 1950s, and many older homes in Turvey Park and Mount Austin sit on strip footings designed without a proper reactivity assessment. The soil in those areas — mainly Quaternary alluvium over weathered Silurian metasediments — can move 40–60 mm between wet and dry cycles. When new extensions or renovations are added, differential movement between old and new footings becomes a real problem. We've seen cracking in brick veneer within two years where the new slab was poured on Class H clay without stiffening beams. The other risk is uncontrolled fill: Wagga Wagga has plenty of sites where 1–2 m of fill was placed decades ago without compaction records. A shallow foundation on that material will settle unevenly unless the fill is removed, recompacted, or bypassed with deeper bearing. AS 2870 Appendix B gives guidance, but site-specific verification is what makes the difference between a warranty claim and a trouble-free structure.

Our services

Our shallow foundation scope in Wagga Wagga covers the geotechnical inputs that structural engineers need for footing and raft design:

Bearing capacity and settlement analysis

We calculate allowable bearing pressure using limit equilibrium methods with site-specific shear strength data, then cross-check settlement via elastic theory. Output is a design bearing pressure and predicted movement envelope.

AS 2870 site classification for slab design

We determine the site class (S through E) based on soil profile, reactivity index, and depth to rock. This drives the stiffening beam and slab panel dimensions for residential construction across the Riverina.

How much does a shallow foundation design report cost for a single residential lot in Wagga Wagga?

For a standard residential lot with one borehole and laboratory testing, the design report typically falls between AU$3.280 and AU$4.520 depending on access conditions and the number of samples required for reactivity testing.

What is the difference between a stiffened raft and a conventional strip footing on reactive clay?

A stiffened raft uses edge beams and internal beams cast integrally with the slab to resist differential movement from reactive clay. Strip footings rely on uniform bearing and do not manage differential heave — they are only suitable for Class S or M sites. On Wagga Wagga's Class H clays, a stiffened raft is standard practice per AS 2870.

Do you need to test the soil before designing a footing extension on an existing home?

Yes. The existing footing and the new extension will sit on soil with potentially different moisture conditions and compaction history. We drill alongside the existing structure, log the profile, and test for reactivity to ensure the new footing design matches or accommodates the expected movement of the original slab.
