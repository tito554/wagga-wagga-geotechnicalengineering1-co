The alluvial flats near the Murrumbidgee River in central Wagga Wagga present a completely different permeability profile than the weathered granite slopes up on Willans Hill. In the river corridor, clean sands and gravels transmit groundwater rapidly, demanding high-frequency Lefranc tests to nail down the hydraulic conductivity before dewatering begins. On the elevated southern side of the city, fractured rock with tight joints often controls the water table, shifting the investigation toward Lugeon packer testing. With over 64,000 people spread across a floodplain that has seen record events like the 2012 inundation, groundwater behavior here isn't an academic exercise, it drives foundation design, basement waterproofing, and earth retention costs. We run these field permeability tests under AS 1726 protocols, giving contractors and dam owners the numbers they need to size pumps, design cut-off walls, and avoid blowout during deep excavations.

A single Lugeon value above 15 in fractured Wagga Wagga Granite can triple the required grout take if not identified before construction.

Technical details of the service in Wagga Wagga

We recently supervised a Lugeon program for a proposed detention basin north of Lake Albert, where the weathered Wagga Wagga Granite showed highly variable fracture connectivity. Five NQ boreholes were drilled to 18 m depth, each with double-packer isolation at 3 m intervals. The test sequence ran five pressure stages per interval, following Houlsby’s method to distinguish laminar flow from fracture dilation or erosion. Data from two intervals revealed hyperpermeable zones exceeding 40 Lugeons—practically open conduits in the rock mass—requiring a revised grout curtain design. For the silty clay overburden, we applied variable-head Lefranc tests using a slotted PVC screen and clean sand filter, recording recovery with a pressure transducer to 1 mm resolution. These numbers fed directly into a Plaxis 2D seepage model that saved the client $60,000 in unnecessary grouting by targeting only the critical horizons. Critical ground factors in Wagga Wagga

Wagga Wagga sits at roughly 147 m elevation on the Murrumbidgee floodplain, and the 2012 flood peaked at over 10 m on the local gauge, saturating the riverbank sediments for weeks. A dewatering plan built on guessed permeability values in these post-flood conditions is a financial ticking clock. Underestimating the hydraulic conductivity by one order of magnitude can double the required well points and add weeks to a construction programme. Top questions

What is the difference between a Lefranc test and a Lugeon test?

A Lefranc test measures permeability in soil or very soft rock, typically using a slotted standpipe and variable or constant head methods. A Lugeon test is specifically for fractured rock: a double packer isolates a section of the borehole and water is injected under pressure in stages to determine the rock mass hydraulic conductivity. In Wagga Wagga, we often use Lefranc in the river alluvium and Lugeon in the underlying granite.

How much does a field permeability test cost in Wagga Wagga?

A single Lefranc or Lugeon test package typically ranges from AU$940 to AU$1,520, depending on borehole depth, number of test intervals, and access conditions. The final quote includes the drill rig, packer system, transducer logging, and the engineering report.

How many Lugeon stages are required per test interval?

We run five pressure stages per interval: low, medium, high, medium, low. This ascending-descending pattern reveals whether the fracture network is dilating, eroding, or filling during injection. It is the standard method established by Houlsby and required for dam foundation assessment in Australia.

Can you test permeability in existing monitoring wells?

Yes, we can perform Lefranc-type variable-head tests in existing PVC monitoring wells if the screen and filter pack are in good condition. However, for Lugeon testing, we require an open borehole in rock to set the double packer. We inspect the well first to confirm it is suitable.

What value of hydraulic conductivity triggers the need for a cut-off wall in Wagga Wagga?

There is no single trigger value; it depends on the hydraulic gradient and the allowable seepage for your project. In the Murrumbidgee floodplain, k values above 1x10^-5 m/s in a gravel layer at foundation level often warrant a cut-off or deep soil mixing barrier. We interpret the test data alongside your structural engineer’s tolerance criteria.
