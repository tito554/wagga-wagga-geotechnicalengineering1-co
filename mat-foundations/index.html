When AS 2870 classifies a site as H1 or H2 due to highly reactive clay—common across Wagga Wagga’s Murrumbidgee floodplain margins—a conventional stiffened strip footing often reaches its practical limits. The design logic shifts toward a fully integrated raft or mat foundation, which acts as a rigid plate to distribute differential movement rather than resisting it locally. In suburbs like Estella or Boorooma, where deep alluvial clay profiles exhibit characteristic gilgai microrelief, we specify post-tensioned slabs with thickened edge beams and internal stiffening ribs aligned to the predicted mound shape. The computational model integrates soil-structure interaction parameters derived from site-specific triaxial testing under consolidated-undrained conditions, ensuring the section capacity exceeds the moment demand induced by an assumed H2 edge heave of 75 mm as per AS 2870-2011. This approach transforms the foundation from a passive element into an engineered system that accommodates seasonal volumetric change without compromising superstructure serviceability.

A raft slab on Wagga Wagga's H2 clays must be engineered as a stiffened raft with sufficient section depth to bridge a 75 mm edge heave—prescriptive M-class designs will not survive the first full wet-dry cycle.

Technical details of the service in Wagga Wagga

The Quaternary alluvium underlying Wagga Wagga consists predominantly of the Shepparton Formation: interbedded clays, silts, and fine sands deposited by the ancestral Murrumbidgee system. The upper 2.0–3.5 m—the critical zone for foundation performance—is dominated by high-plasticity clay (CH) with liquid limits frequently exceeding 60% and plasticity indices above 30%. This mineralogy, rich in smectite, drives a shrink-swell behaviour that produces characteristic surface movement (ys) values between 40 and 75 mm across a typical suburban block. Our structural response to these conditions integrates the site-specific Iss from Atterberg limits testing with a finite element slab model that simulates the distorted mound shape defined in AS 2870-2011 Appendix B. The resulting tendon layout and beam section are calibrated not to the average condition but to the 90th percentile edge heave scenario, providing serviceability protection that a prescriptive M-class slab cannot deliver on Wagga's reactive clays.

Critical ground factors in Wagga Wagga

The risk profile changes markedly between Wagga Wagga's established central grid and the newer greenfield subdivisions pushing into the eastern slopes. In the older residential areas near the Murrumbidgee River, alluvial silty clays with moderate reactivity (M–H1) and a relatively stable water table at 2.5–3.0 m depth produce predictable seasonal movement patterns. Compare this to the elevated terrain around Forest Hill, where deeply weathered S-type granodiorite has generated residual clay profiles exceeding 4.0 m thickness with Iss values above 6.0%—an H2 classification is almost guaranteed. Design on these sites demands a fully suspended slab with void formers and deepened edge beams extending beyond the zone of seasonal moisture fluctuation. Ignoring this contrast and applying a standard Wagga design to a hillside block introduces unacceptably high risk of differential movement exceeding 30 mm, which will manifest as partition cracking and door binding within the first two wet-dry cycles.

Our services

Our Wagga Wagga raft and mat foundation design workflow integrates three interdependent phases, each calibrated to the Riverina's specific geotechnical environment. Every phase references current Australian Standards and is executed by engineers familiar with local council requirements.

Geotechnical Site Characterisation

We conduct deep borehole logging to 3.0–4.5 m depth across the building footprint, recovering undisturbed Shelby tube samples from the reactive clay horizon. Laboratory testing quantifies the shrink-swell index (Iss), Atterberg limits, and suction profile, forming the basis for a site classification to AS 2870-2011 Section 2.

Structural Design of Post-Tensioned Raft Slabs

Using finite element modelling, we design post-tensioned slabs with ribbed stiffening beams at 4–6 m centres. The tendon layout is optimised for the characteristic mound shape, with unbonded monostrand systems stressed to 80% of ultimate tensile strength. All designs meet AS 3600:2018 ductility requirements for crack control under edge heave conditions.

Construction-Phase Inspection & Void Form Specification

We specify degradable void formers beneath internal beams to create a permanent separation from the expansive subgrade. During construction, our team verifies concrete placement, tendon profile tolerance, and moisture-conditioning of the founding material prior to the slab pour.

The final figure depends on slab area, number of stiffening ribs, and complexity of the mound shape assumed in the analysis.

How does AS 2870 classify reactive sites and why does it matter for raft design?

AS 2870-2011 classifies sites from A (stable) to E (extreme) based on expected surface movement (ys). In Wagga Wagga, medium to highly reactive clays commonly yield H1 (40–60 mm) or H2 (60–75 mm) classifications. This classification directly dictates the edge heave distance, mound shape, and the required beam depth and reinforcement in a raft slab—a standard stiffened raft designed for an M class site would be structurally inadequate on an H2 profile.

What soil testing is required before designing a mat foundation in the Riverina?

A full investigation to AS 1726-2017 should include boreholes at minimum 3.0 m depth at each corner of the proposed footprint, with undisturbed sampling of the reactive clay layer. Laboratory testing must determine the shrink-swell index (Iss) via shrink-swell core test, Atterberg limits, linear shrinkage, and soil suction profile. In some cases we supplement with in-situ permeability testing to assess drainage characteristics that influence moisture equilibrium beneath the slab.

How long does the raft foundation design process take from investigation to approved documentation?

A typical timeline runs four to five weeks: one week for fieldwork and sampling, two weeks for laboratory testing and site classification, and one to two weeks for structural modelling, drafting, and coordination with the building certifier. Council approval times in Wagga Wagga vary separately from our design schedule.
